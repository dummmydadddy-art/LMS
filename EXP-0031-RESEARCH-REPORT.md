## 1. Executive Summary

The independent scientific and statistical audit of EXP-0030 revealed a critical vulnerability in the premise verification pipeline: **35 of 46 false refutations (76.09%) originated in the Tier-1 deterministic fast path**. The legacy Tier-1 engine evaluated keyword antonyms (e.g. `mutable`/`immutable`, `main axis`/`cross axis`) and predicate negation keywords using raw string-level proximity, without establishing syntactic clause attachment, entity-subject alignment, or proposition-level polarity concordance. Furthermore, Pass-2 asymmetric calibration produced zero decision changes, proving that calibration cannot fix upstream false contradictions generated by an ungrounded deterministic shortcut.

EXP-0031 resolved this fundamental defect by architecting a **5-point structural validation pipeline** directly inside `validateTier1Contradiction()` within `backend/rag_premise_verifier.php`. Whenever a lexical antonym or predicate negation pattern is detected, the candidate contradiction must satisfy rigorous syntactic and structural tests before a fast-path `REFUTED` verdict is emitted. If any validation test fails, the engine conservatively downgrades the match to `NO_DETERMINISTIC_REFUTATION` and falls through cleanly to the pre-filter gate and Tier-2 NLI semantic inference.

## 4. Forensic Root Cause & Theoretical Motivation

In EXP-0030, an independent audit revealed that 35 of 46 false refutations (76.09%) occurred on the Tier-1 fast path. Forensic analysis (`EXP-0031-TIER1-FORENSICS.md`) traced these failures to 8 distinct structural and syntactic root causes:

1. **Entity Sibling Collision (37.0% of failures):**
   Retrieved curriculum bullets frequently define contrasting concepts within the same chunk (e.g. `var` vs `let` vs `const`, or `flex-direction: row` vs `column`). When a query asks about `row`, the legacy Tier-1 engine saw `column` in the same chunk and fired an antonym contradiction, ignoring that the evidence was contrasting two siblings rather than refuting the query's subject.
2. **Predicate Target Mismatch (21.7% of failures):**
   The query and evidence shared antonym words, but applied them to different technical properties or objects (e.g. query discusses `margin-bottom` while evidence discusses `padding-top`).
3. **Polarity Concordance Fallacy (15.2% of failures):**
   The query asserted a negative proposition (e.g. "Does `const` prevent reassignment?") and the evidence also affirmed that negative proposition ("`const` cannot be reassigned"). The legacy engine matched `cannot` as a negation keyword and declared contradiction against the query's verb `reassignment`, inverting agreement into contradiction.
4. **Subordinate Condition Blindness (8.7% of failures):**
   Antonyms or negation terms appeared inside conditional or subordinate clauses (e.g. "unless", "if not specified", "except when"), but were treated as unconditional contradictions of the main clause.
5. **Disjunctive Compatibility (6.5% of failures):**
   Queries asking about alternative behaviors (e.g. "X or Y") where evidence stated Y, causing the legacy engine to treat the presence of X's antonym as a refutation.
6. **Non-Exclusive Property Attribute (4.3% of failures):**
   Properties that are complementary rather than mutually exclusive.
7. **Double Negation Inversion (4.3% of failures):**
   Double negatives in complex sentences improperly resolved.
8. **Definitional Distinction Overreach (2.2% of failures):**
   Taxonomic distinctions misinterpreted as mutual exclusions.

## 5. 5-Point Structural Validation Pipeline Architecture

To systematically eliminate these 8 failure modes, EXP-0031 introduced a modular 5-point structural validation pipeline encapsulated in `validateTier1Contradiction()` within `backend/rag_premise_verifier.php`:

```
Candidate Lexical Opposition / Predicate Negation Hit
                           │
                           ▼
  [Step 1] Entity & Sibling Alignment Gate
      Are focal entities congruent? Sibling separation clean?
                           │ PASS
                           ▼
  [Step 2] Predicate Target Matching
      Do verbs/predicates govern identical semantic targets?
                           │ PASS
                           ▼
  [Step 3] Negation Scope & Polarity Concordance
      Are negative polarities in agreement or in opposition?
                           │ PASS
                           ▼
  [Step 4] Subordinate & Conditional Scope Gate
      Is the negation restricted to a conditional clause?
                           │ PASS
                           ▼
  [Step 5] Deterministic Contradiction Confirmed -> Emit REFUTED
                           │
                           └─► If ANY step FAILS:
                               Downgrade to NO_DETERMINISTIC_REFUTATION
                               Fall through cleanly to Tier-2 NLI
```

### Component Details:

- **Step 1: Entity & Sibling Alignment Gate (`checkEntityAlignment`):**
  Identifies technical subject entities in both query and evidence line. Verifies that the antonym pair is applied to the *same* subject entity. If the evidence line introduces an explicit sibling bullet (e.g. `- const:` vs `- let:`), cross-talk is rejected.
- **Step 2: Predicate Target Matching (`checkPredicateTargetMatch`):**
  Extracts the direct grammatical object or modifier of the predicate verb. Ensures that opposites apply to the identical property (e.g., verifying that `vertical` vs `horizontal` applies to the main axis, not to cross-axis or alignment).
- **Step 3: Negation Scope & Polarity Concordance (`checkPolarityConcordance`):**
  Evaluates proposition polarity. If the query asks "Does X NOT do Y?" and the evidence states "X does NOT do Y", the polarities concord ($(-1) \times (-1) = +1$, agreement), preventing false contradiction.
- **Step 4: Subordinate & Conditional Scope Gate (`checkSubordinateScope`):**
  Detects subordinate conjunctions (`unless`, `except`, `provided that`, `if false`). Negations residing strictly within subordinate boundaries are prevented from negating the primary assertion.
- **Step 5: Conservative Fall-Through to Tier-2 NLI:**
  Crucially, failing a Tier-1 structural test does *not* declare `SUPPORTED`; instead, it declares `NO_DETERMINISTIC_REFUTATION`. The claim is handed over to the pre-filter gate and Tier-2 NLI for evidence-grounded semantic inference.

## 14. Statistical Significance Analysis (McNemar Test)

To verify that the accuracy improvement from 58.00% (E0) to 62.80% (E4) is statistically genuine, an Edwards-corrected McNemar test was conducted on all 500 matched pairs:

### Contingency Table: E0 (Baseline) vs. E4 (Candidate)

```
                       E4 Correct          E4 Incorrect          Total
E0 Correct                276                   14                290
E0 Incorrect               38                  172                210
Total                     314                  186                500
```

- **Discordant Pairs:**
  - $b$ (E0 correct, E4 incorrect): **14 cases**
  - $c$ (E0 incorrect, E4 correct): **38 cases**
  - Total discordant ($n_{disc} = b + c$): **52 cases**
- **Edwards Continuity-Corrected Chi-Squared:**
  $$\chi^2 = \frac{(|b - c| - 1)^2}{b + c} = \frac{(|14 - 38| - 1)^2}{52} = \frac{23^2}{52} = \frac{529}{52} = \mathbf{10.1731}$$
- **Exact Two-Tailed Binomial $p$-value:**
  $$p = \sum_{k=0}^{14} \binom{52}{k} (0.5)^{52} \times 2 = \mathbf{1.1951 \times 10^{-3}} \; (p < 0.0012)$$
- **Significance Level:**
  - Statistically significant at $\alpha = 0.05$: **YES**
  - Statistically significant at $\alpha = 0.01$: **YES** ($p = 0.001195 < 0.01$)

The empirical lift is overwhelmingly statistically significant, with $c$ exceeding $b$ by nearly $3:1$.

## 20. Scientific Threats to Validity & Limitations

1. **Local LLM Parametric Bias:**
   Tier-2 relies on `llama3.2:latest`. In 23 cases, zero-shot NLI returned overconfident contradictions on subtle phrasing despite high temperature damping ($T = 0.0$).
2. **Grammatical Complexity in Technical English:**
   Regex-based syntactic heuristics for subordinate clauses cannot capture deeply nested parse trees as accurately as dependency parsers (e.g. spaCy), leaving 12 residual Tier-1 false alarms.
3. **Curriculum Asymmetry (Missing Antitheses):**
   Technical documentation typically states what a feature *does*, rarely enumerating all things it *does not* do. This creates an intrinsic ceiling on refutation recall for false premises.
